The four things that catch people out

Nights go wrong in Bangkok not because the city is dangerous, but because people walk in without knowing the rules. Almost every problem a foreign visitor runs into falls into one of four buckets.

1. An opaque bill
ordered without seeing prices
2. Vapes
illegal to even carry in Thailand
3. Dry days and hours
the customer can be fined too
4. Unlicensed venues
no way to check the licence
Avoid those four and
the night in Asok ends without incident

The laws and reporting below are the ones sourced in this site's news section. The figures are the statutory maximums; enforcement in practice varies. Rules and crackdowns move quickly, so confirm with the venue on the night.

Three situations where it goes wrong

Bill disputes and police problems concentrate in three specific moments.

1. Sitting down without seeing a menu
where most bill disputes start

You are shown to a table, you toast, you buy a few drinks for the woman next to you — and at no point have you seen a price. That is the dangerous state. Orders stack up at unknown unit prices and the total is a surprise at the end. The fix takes ten seconds: ask for the price list the moment you sit down. A licensed venue can always produce one. A venue that cannot has just told you to leave while it is still free to do so.

2. Looking for a venue after 2am
the hour that funnels people into unlicensed bars

Asok and Sukhumvit close at 2am. The 4am licence covers only the designated zones — Silom/Patpong, RCA to New Phetchaburi, Ratchadaphisek. There are exceptions, such as licensed venues inside hotels, but otherwise a place still trading around Asok after 2am is one whose licensing position you cannot verify for yourself. Walking into a venue whose prices and licence you cannot check, several drinks in, is the worst possible way to enter one. If you want to keep going, take a taxi to a designated zone instead.

3. Landing on a Buddhist holy day
the days the shops stop selling

Thailand enforces statutory dry days. The last were 29-30 July 2026; the next is Ok Phansa on Monday 26 October 2026. On those days convenience stores and most standalone bars stop selling, while licensed hotels, international-airport terminals and licensed entertainment venues inside designated tourist areas can keep serving. If your trip lands on one, build the night around a hotel bar or a licensed venue, or check the holiday calendar before you book.

Four moves that keep the bill clean

Each takes seconds, and together they remove nearly all of the uncertainty.

  1. Ask for the price list as you sit down — Check four things: cover or table charge, set fee, drink prices, nomination fee. If a venue will only tell you verbally and cannot show a list, leaving right then is the cheapest decision you will make all night.
  2. Fix your lady-drink number in advance — This is what inflates a bill more than anything else. You do not have to refuse them — just set the ceiling before you start, and remember it multiplies by the number of people at the table.
  3. Check whether service charge and VAT are extra — At most venues they are added on top: 10% plus 7%, about 17% together. Budget at 1.2x the listed price and the total will never surprise you.
  4. Read the bill before you pay — Look for items you did not order and for service charge or VAT applied twice. Ask at the table and it gets fixed; ask on the street and it does not.

Five ways to spot a safe venue

Since the Lat Phrao fire in July 2026, Bangkok has been running a citywide sweep of fire safety and operating licences. By 23 August, 1,040 venues had been checked: 280 were rated green, 639 were rated yellow or orange and told to make improvements, and 121 were rated red. Most venues, in other words, are working through remediation — and the standard for choosing one has genuinely shifted in the last few months. Sources: The Standard, Bangkok Post.

  1. Is the operating licence displayed? — Licensed entertainment venues display one. Of the 121 venues rated red in the sweep, 81 were operating without a licence at all.
  2. Check the fire exit when you sit down — It takes ten seconds and matters most in basements and upper floors. In the Lat Phrao fire, the emergency exits were reported to have been blocked by beer crates and tables.
  3. Will they show you a price list? — A venue that can state its prices bills transparently. The same test that protects your wallet also sorts the properly run venues from the rest.
  4. Is there English or Japanese-speaking staff? — Being able to confirm the pricing and the terms in a language you speak removes almost every misunderstanding-driven dispute. It matters most with a group.
  5. Can you reach them in advance? — A venue you can book or check by LINE or phone is one whose operation is visible. It also saves you from arriving at a venue that is temporarily closed for inspection.
